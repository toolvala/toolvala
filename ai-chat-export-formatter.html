Why AI Chat Exports Need Cleaning Up

    
More people now do real work inside AI chat windows — drafting outlines, debugging code, planning trips, or thinking through decisions — and then want to save that conversation somewhere permanent: a document, a blog post, a wiki page, or personal notes. The problem is that copying text directly out of a chat interface usually brings along inconsistent line breaks, missing speaker labels, and formatting quirks that don't survive the paste into a Word document or Markdown editor cleanly.

    
This tool exists to close that small but repeated gap: paste in the raw conversation, choose how you want it labeled and formatted, and get back something you can drop straight into whatever you're building next.

A Note on Using AI Chat Content Publicly

    
If you plan to publish formatted AI chat output — as a blog post, guide, or documentation — it's worth pairing it with a short disclosure noting that the content originated from or was assisted by an AI conversation, and reviewing it for accuracy before publishing, since AI responses can contain mistakes even when they read smoothly.


    
Privacy Note

    
This tool runs entirely in your browser. The text you paste is processed locally with JavaScript and is never sent to a server, logged, or stored — closing the tab clears it completely.


    
Frequently Asked Questions

    
What does this tool do?
It takes a pasted AI conversation and cleans up spacing, labels each speaker turn, and outputs readable Markdown or plain text.

    
Does this tool store or send my conversation anywhere?
No. Formatting happens entirely in your browser — nothing is uploaded to a server.

    
Can I format ChatGPT, Claude, and Gemini exports?
Yes, it works on any pasted text conversation regardless of which AI tool it came from.

    
Will this remove AI hallucinations or errors?
No — it only cleans formatting and structure, not the accuracy of the content itself.
