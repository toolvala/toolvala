What is a Headphone Test?

A Headphone Test lets you instantly verify that both ear channels of your headphones or earbuds are working correctly, and that stereo panning (sound moving between left and right) functions as expected. This free online tool uses your browser's Web Audio API to generate test tones routed specifically to each channel, plus an automatic panning sweep that lets you hear sound smoothly move from one ear to the other — a great way to catch subtle imbalances that a simple "does it make sound" check might miss.

This test works for wired headphones, Bluetooth headphones, true wireless earbuds, and gaming headsets alike, as long as they're properly connected and selected as your device's active audio output.

Why Stereo Panning Matters

Stereo panning is what creates a sense of space and direction in audio — footsteps moving across a room in a game, an instrument coming from one side in a song mix, or simply hearing a caller's voice centered clearly during a call. If your headphones can't reproduce panning smoothly and evenly, it usually points to either a hardware imbalance between the two drivers or a cable/connection issue affecting one channel more than the other.

Tips for Better Headphone Care and Audio Quality

  • Store cables without tight coiling: Repeated sharp bends near the jack are a leading cause of internal wire damage over time.
  • Clean ear cushions and mesh regularly: Dust and earwax buildup can noticeably muffle sound output.
  • Check the audio balance setting: Most operating systems have a left/right balance slider that can accidentally get shifted away from center.
  • Fully charge wireless earbuds evenly: Uneven battery levels between two wireless earbuds can sometimes cause one side to sound weaker or cut out first.
  • Avoid maximum volume for long periods: This protects both your hearing and the longevity of the headphone drivers.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why does the auto pan sweep matter?

It lets you hear a smooth, continuous transition of sound from one ear to the other, which helps reveal subtle imbalances or dropouts that simple left/right button tests alone might not catch.

My wireless earbuds sound unbalanced - is it the earbuds or my phone?

Try this test with different headphones on the same device, and try your original earbuds on a different device. If the imbalance follows the earbuds, the hardware is likely the cause; if it follows the device, check your device's audio balance setting.

Can I use this test with a gaming headset?

Yes, gaming headsets work the same way as regular headphones for this test, as long as they're selected as your active audio output device.

Is it safe to run this test at high volume?

We recommend starting at a lower volume using the slider provided and increasing gradually, both to protect your hearing and to avoid an unexpectedly loud test tone.
