Why Greenhouse Area Calculations Matter

Getting greenhouse (polyhouse) area right feeds directly into two of the biggest cost decisions in a protected cultivation project: how much covering material (typically UV-stabilized polyethylene film) to buy, and how much usable growing floor space the structure actually provides. Covering material is priced by area and typically sold in fixed-width rolls, so an accurate area estimate — including a sensible wastage allowance for overlaps and future patch repairs — avoids both costly over-ordering and disruptive mid-project shortfalls.

This calculator handles the two most common commercial greenhouse shapes: the tunnel (Quonset), with its continuous semi-circular arched roof, and the gable-roof design, with two flat, angled roof panels meeting at a central ridge.

Tunnel (Quonset) Greenhouse Formula

A tunnel greenhouse's cross-section is a semicircle, with the radius equal to half the greenhouse width. The roof covering area is the arc length of that semicircle multiplied by the greenhouse's length, and the two semi-circular end walls add up to one full circle's worth of area.

Roof Area = π × (Width ÷ 2) × Length
End Wall Area (both ends) = π × (Width ÷ 2)²

Gable-Roof Greenhouse Formula

A gable roof's two panels are each a rectangle whose width is the "slant length" from the side wall to the ridge, calculated using the Pythagorean theorem from half the greenhouse width and the height difference between the ridge and the side wall.

Slant Length = √[(Width ÷ 2)² + (Ridge Height − Side Wall Height)²]
Roof Area = 2 × Slant Length × Greenhouse Length
End Wall Area (both ends, incl. gable triangles) = 2 × [(Width × Side Height) + (0.5 × Width × (Ridge Height − Side Height))]

Choosing Between Tunnel and Gable Designs

  • Tunnel (Quonset): Generally simpler and cheaper to construct, with good structural strength against wind due to its curved shape, though headroom near the sides is more limited.
  • Gable roof: Offers more usable headroom throughout and can better accommodate taller crops or overhead equipment like hanging trellis systems, but typically costs more to build due to the more complex frame.
This calculator provides a general geometric estimate for planning purposes. Actual film requirements should be confirmed with your greenhouse fabricator or supplier, accounting for door and vent openings, roll widths, and local wind-load design requirements.

Tips for Ordering Covering Material

  • Always add a wastage allowance. 10–15% extra covers seam overlaps, installation cutting waste, and future patching material.
  • Check roll widths before ordering. Poly film typically comes in fixed roll widths, which may require the covering to be pieced together with overlapping seams rather than a single continuous sheet.
  • Consider UV-stabilized film. Standard polyethylene degrades quickly under continuous sun exposure; UV-stabilized film typically lasts several years longer.
  • Plan for replacement cycles. Most greenhouse film needs replacement every 3 to 5 years depending on climate and film quality — factor this into long-term cost planning.

Final Thoughts

A few accurate dimension inputs go a long way toward avoiding both wasted covering material and unexpected shortfalls mid-installation. Use this calculator at the planning stage of any new greenhouse project, and recalculate whenever you're evaluating design changes like widening the structure or switching from a tunnel to a gable-roof design.
