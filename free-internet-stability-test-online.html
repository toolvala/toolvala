What is an Internet Stability Test?

An Internet Stability Test measures how consistent your connection's latency is over time, rather than just checking a single snapshot speed. This free online tool sends repeated small requests over a 30-second window and plots each response time on a live animated graph, revealing latency spikes, jitter (variation in latency), and packet loss (failed requests) that a one-time speed test alone wouldn't show.

Connection stability matters enormously for real-time activities like video calls, online gaming, and livestreaming — even a connection with high raw download speed can feel choppy or laggy if latency fluctuates wildly or packets get dropped intermittently.

Understanding Jitter and Packet Loss

MetricWhat It MeansGood Range
Average LatencyTypical round-trip response time in millisecondsUnder 50ms excellent, under 100ms good
JitterVariation between consecutive latency measurementsUnder 10ms is ideal for gaming and calls
Packet LossPercentage of requests that failed to complete0% is ideal; above 1-2% can cause noticeable issues

High jitter causes the "choppy" or "robotic" audio quality sometimes heard during video calls, since inconsistent packet arrival times disrupt smooth audio/video reconstruction. Packet loss can cause frozen video frames, dropped calls, or rubber-banding in online games, even when average speed and latency look fine on paper.

Why Stability Matters More Than Raw Speed for Some Activities

  • Video calls: Consistent low latency and jitter matter more than raw download speed for smooth, natural-feeling conversations.
  • Online gaming: Stable, low ping prevents "lag spikes" and rubber-banding, often mattering more than high bandwidth.
  • Livestreaming: Stable upload performance prevents stream buffering and quality drops for viewers.
  • VoIP calls: Jitter and packet loss directly cause choppy or garbled audio, even on connections with plenty of raw bandwidth.

Tips to Improve Connection Stability

  1. Use a wired ethernet connection for the most stable, consistent latency, especially for gaming or important calls.
  2. Position your router centrally and minimize physical obstructions between your device and the router if using Wi-Fi.
  3. Restart your router periodically to clear temporary connection issues and refresh the connection.
  4. Limit simultaneous heavy usage like large downloads or 4K streaming while doing latency-sensitive activities.
  5. Contact your ISP if instability persists across multiple tests and devices, as this may indicate a line or infrastructure issue.

Frequently Asked Questions

What's a good jitter value?

Under 10ms is considered excellent for gaming and video calls, while jitter above 30ms often becomes noticeable as choppy audio or inconsistent game responsiveness.

My packet loss shows 0% but I still experience lag - why?

Lag can also result from high average latency or jitter even without outright packet loss, or from issues specific to a particular app or game server rather than your general connection.

Should I test on Wi-Fi or wired connection?

Testing both separately can help you determine whether instability is related to your Wi-Fi setup specifically or your broader internet connection.

How long should I run the stability test?

This test runs for 30 seconds by default, which is generally enough to reveal meaningful patterns, though running it multiple times at different times of day gives a fuller picture.
