Why Accurate Pesticide Mixing Matters

Getting pesticide concentration right in the spray tank sits right at the intersection of effectiveness, cost, and safety. Too dilute, and the application may fail to control the target pest, disease or weed — wasting the labour and fuel spent spraying while also contributing to resistance developing in the pest population over repeated sub-lethal exposures. Too concentrated, and you risk crop damage (phytotoxicity), unnecessary cost from over-application, and potential violations of legal maximum residue limits on the harvested produce. This calculator handles the arithmetic behind converting a label's recommended dose into an exact quantity for your specific tank and field, so the only variable left to get right is following the label's actual recommended rate.

Two Common Ways Doses Are Given

Dose per Litre of Water

Many product labels state a simple dilution rate, such as "2 ml per litre of water." This is the most direct method — multiply that rate by your tank's capacity to get the total product for one tank load.

Product per Tank = Dose per Litre × Tank Capacity (litres)

Dose per Hectare

Other labels, particularly for herbicides and some field-scale products, state a dose per unit area instead — such as "500 ml per hectare." To use this with a fixed-size tank, you first need to know your sprayer's typical spray volume per hectare (how many litres of total spray solution you apply per hectare at your normal walking speed and nozzle setting), then work out the equivalent concentration per litre.

Concentration per Litre = Dose per Hectare ÷ Spray Volume per Hectare
Product per Tank = Concentration per Litre × Tank Capacity

This calculator also works out your total spray volume and number of tank loads for the whole field, along with total product needed, once you enter your field area.

Safe Mixing Practice

  • Fill the tank partially before adding product. A common safe sequence is to add roughly half to two-thirds of the water first, then the measured pesticide, mix thoroughly, and top up to the final volume — this reduces splash risk and helps the product disperse evenly rather than settling.
  • Use dedicated measuring equipment. A proper measuring cup or syringe marked for the product avoids both under- and over-dosing from eyeballing quantities.
  • Never mix products without confirming label compatibility. Tank-mixing incompatible chemicals can cause reduced effectiveness, unwanted reactions, or crop damage — only combine products where the labels explicitly confirm it's safe.
  • Mix only what you'll use that day. Diluted pesticide solutions generally shouldn't be stored for extended periods, both for effectiveness and safety reasons.

Personal Safety When Handling Pesticides

  • Wear the personal protective equipment specified on the label — this often includes gloves, eye protection, and sometimes a respirator or coveralls, depending on the product's toxicity class.
  • Mix outdoors in a well-ventilated area, away from wells, ponds, or other water sources that could be contaminated by spills or runoff.
  • Never eat, drink, or smoke while mixing or applying pesticide, and wash hands and exposed skin thoroughly afterward.
  • Store leftover concentrate in its original, clearly labelled container, out of reach of children and away from food storage areas.
This calculator performs the mixing arithmetic only and does not provide pest management, dosage safety, or product recommendations. Always follow the exact dose, timing, safety precautions, and re-entry interval specified on the product label, and consult a qualified agronomist or extension officer with any uncertainty.

Calibrating Your Sprayer First

The per-hectare method depends on knowing your sprayer's actual spray volume per hectare, which varies with walking speed, nozzle type, pressure, and boom width. A simple calibration check — timing how long it takes to empty a known tank volume while spraying a measured area at your normal working pace — gives a far more accurate spray volume figure than assuming a generic textbook number, and is worth doing at the start of each spraying season or whenever equipment changes.

Final Thoughts

Whether your label gives a simple per-litre rate or a per-hectare rate, converting it into an exact, confident measurement for your specific tank removes guesswork from one of the most safety-sensitive tasks on the farm. Recalculate whenever you switch products, change tank size, or recalibrate your sprayer, and always keep the product label as your final authority on dose and safety.
