Why cups and grams don’t match

A cup measures volume and grams measure weight. The same cup holds about 85 g of cocoa powder but 340 g of honey, because honey is far denser. That is why a flat “1 cup = X grams” rule never works and why this converter uses a separate density for every ingredient.

Cup sizes also differ by country. A US cup is 236.6 ml, a metric cup is 250 ml, an imperial cup is 284 ml and a Japanese cup is 200 ml. Pick the size your recipe was written for, and the grams update to match.

How to measure a cup accurately

  • Flour: fluff it, spoon it into the cup, then level with a knife. Scooping packs in up to 20% extra.
  • Brown sugar: press it firmly into the cup (“packed”).
  • Liquids: use a clear measuring jug and read at eye level.
  • Best of all: weigh in grams and skip the cup.

Frequently asked questions

How many grams are in 1 cup of flour?

One US cup of all-purpose flour is about 125 g. Scooping straight from the bag can pack it to 140–150 g, so spoon and level for accuracy.

How many grams are in 1 cup of sugar?

Granulated sugar is about 200 g per cup, packed brown sugar about 213 g and powdered sugar about 120 g.

Why does a cup of different ingredients weigh differently?

Because a cup is a volume. Dense ingredients such as honey weigh far more per cup than light ones such as cocoa powder or coconut.

Is a cup the same size everywhere?

No. US 236.6 ml, metric 250 ml, imperial 284 ml, Japanese 200 ml. Choose your cup size in the converter.

How many grams are in 1 cup of butter?

About 227 g for a US cup, which equals two sticks or 16 tablespoons.
