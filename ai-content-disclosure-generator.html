Why Disclose AI-Generated Content?

    
As AI writing and image tools have become part of everyday content production, the question of when and how to tell your audience has moved from a nice-to-have to something platforms, regulators, and readers increasingly expect. A disclosure label is a small addition — usually one or two sentences — that states plainly whether and how AI was involved in producing a piece of content.

    
This matters for a few practical reasons. Major video and social platforms have introduced labeling requirements for synthetic or AI-generated media, particularly where it could otherwise be mistaken for something entirely human-made. Search engines have also signaled that transparency and clear sourcing matter more than the mere fact that AI was used. And readers, when they discover undisclosed AI content after the fact, tend to trust the source less going forward — disclosure up front avoids that entirely.


    
What Counts as "AI-Generated" or "AI-Assisted"?

    
There's a real difference between content that was fully produced by an AI tool with light human review, and content where AI played a smaller supporting role — fixing grammar, suggesting a headline, or translating a paragraph. Being specific about which category applies is more useful to readers than a blanket "AI was used somewhere" statement, which is why this generator asks you to pick the closest match rather than offering one generic label.

Disclosure vs Disclaimer

    
A disclosure simply states the fact of AI involvement — "this article was drafted with AI assistance." A disclaimer goes a step further, adding a note about limitations, such as the possibility of factual errors, and often an invitation for readers to flag mistakes. Combining both, as this generator does by default, tends to read as more trustworthy than a bare disclosure alone, since it shows you've thought about accuracy, not just attribution.


    
Frequently Asked Questions

    
What counts as AI-generated content?
Content is generally considered AI-generated or AI-assisted if an AI tool wrote, drafted, edited, translated, or created a substantial part of it, even after a human review.

    
Do I have to disclose AI-generated content?
Requirements vary by platform and region. Many platforms now require or encourage labeling AI-generated content, and disclosure builds trust even where it isn't strictly mandatory.

    
What's the difference between a disclosure and a disclaimer?
A disclosure states that AI was involved. A disclaimer adds a note about possible limitations or errors and often invites readers to report mistakes.

    
Where should I place the disclosure?
Near the top of an article, or in the description for video content — the most visible spot matches most platform requirements.
