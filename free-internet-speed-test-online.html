What is an Internet Speed Test?

An Internet Speed Test measures three key aspects of your internet connection's performance: download speed (how fast data transfers from the internet to your device), upload speed (how fast data transfers from your device to the internet), and ping (the round-trip latency for a small data request). This free online tool runs the entire test directly in your browser, transferring real test data to measure your actual live connection performance rather than relying on your ISP's advertised maximum speed.

Testing your speed regularly helps you verify you're getting the performance you're paying for, troubleshoot slow streaming or video calls, and diagnose whether a performance issue is related to your internet plan, your Wi-Fi setup, or a specific device.

Why Speed Test Results Can Vary

  • Server distance: Test results depend partly on the network distance to the test server being used.
  • Wi-Fi vs wired connection: Wi-Fi typically shows lower and more variable speeds than a direct ethernet connection.
  • Network congestion: Testing during peak hours (evenings) often shows lower speeds than off-peak times.
  • Other active devices: Other devices on your network streaming, downloading, or backing up data reduce available bandwidth during your test.
  • Browser and device performance: Older devices or browsers with many open tabs can slightly limit measured throughput.

Tips for Getting the Most Accurate Results

  1. Close other apps and tabs that might be using bandwidth in the background during the test.
  2. Use a wired connection when possible to test your true internet speed without Wi-Fi interference.
  3. Pause downloads, streaming, and cloud backups on other devices sharing your network.
  4. Run the test multiple times at different times of day for a more complete picture of your typical performance.
  5. Restart your router if results seem unusually low compared to your expected plan speed.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why is my result lower than my internet plan's advertised speed?

Advertised speeds represent a maximum under ideal conditions. Wi-Fi signal strength, network congestion, device limitations, and server distance can all reduce real-world measured speed below the advertised maximum.

Why does my upload speed show lower than download?

Most home internet plans (especially cable and DSL) are asymmetric, meaning they're designed to offer more download bandwidth than upload bandwidth — this is normal and expected for most connection types except symmetric fiber.

Is this test as accurate as dedicated speed test apps?

This browser-based test provides a solid real-world estimate, though results may differ slightly from specialized speed test services due to differences in server infrastructure and testing methodology.

What is a good ping for online gaming?

Under 30ms is considered excellent for competitive gaming, 30-60ms is good for most games, and above 100ms may start to introduce noticeable input lag in fast-paced games.
