Planning Hay Needs for a Feeding Season

Hay remains one of the most widely used forage sources for cattle, horses, sheep, and goats, particularly through dry seasons or winter months when pasture is unavailable. Underestimating hay requirements risks running short mid-season when replacement hay may be more expensive or harder to source, while overestimating ties up cash and storage space unnecessarily. Getting a reliable number ahead of the feeding season — whether you're buying hay or planning your own harvest — makes budgeting and storage planning considerably easier.

This calculator converts your herd size, daily intake, and feeding period into total hay weight needed, then divides that by your chosen bale type's typical weight to give a practical bale count you can actually order or plan storage space for.

The Hay Bale Calculation Formula

Total Hay Needed (kg) = Number of Animals × Daily Intake per Animal (kg) × Feeding Days
Total with Buffer = Total Hay × (1 + Buffer% ÷ 100)
Bales Needed = Total with Buffer ÷ Bale Weight (rounded up)

How to Use This Calculator

  1. Enter your number of animals and expected daily hay intake per animal.
  2. Enter your planned feeding period in days.
  3. Set a waste/spoilage buffer — 10 to 20% is a common general range to account for feeding losses and storage spoilage.
  4. Choose a bale type preset, or enter your own known bale weight.
  5. Optionally enter a price per bale to see an estimated total cost.
  6. Tap "Calculate Bales Needed" to see total hay required and the number of bales to source.

Estimating Daily Hay Intake

A widely used general guideline is that grazing livestock consume roughly 1.5 to 2.5 percent of their body weight in hay dry matter per day when hay is their primary or sole forage source. A 450 kilogram animal at 2% body weight would need about 9 kilograms of hay daily, though actual intake varies with hay quality, animal activity level, weather, and whether other feed sources supplement the diet.

Choosing the Right Bale Type

  • Small square bales: Easy to hand-carry and portion precisely, well suited to smaller herds or hand-feeding situations, but more labour-intensive per tonne of hay handled.
  • Round bales: Efficient for larger herds and mechanized feeding, but require equipment to move and are harder to portion precisely for smaller groups.
  • Large square bales: Offer efficient storage stacking and transport for large operations, similarly requiring equipment to handle.
This calculator provides a general planning estimate. Actual bale weights vary considerably even within the same nominal type, and actual intake depends on animal size, activity, and hay quality — weigh sample bales and monitor body condition to refine your real-world numbers.

Storage Tips to Reduce Waste

  • Keep bales off bare ground. Pallets, gravel pads, or raised platforms prevent moisture wicking up into the bottom of stored bales.
  • Cover from rain. A roof or waterproof tarp significantly reduces spoilage compared to fully exposed outdoor storage.
  • Allow airflow between stacks. Tightly packed, poorly ventilated storage increases the risk of mould and, in worst cases, spontaneous heating.
  • Use older hay first. Rotate stock so bales aren't stored so long that nutritional quality degrades before use.

Final Thoughts

Turning a herd's daily forage needs into a concrete bale count and budget removes much of the seasonal guesswork from hay planning. Use this calculator at the start of each feeding season, and revisit it whenever herd size, feeding duration, or bale sourcing options change.
